# Tiny Steps
## Tagline: Small steps for big changes

## Game Concept

Tiny Steps is a time management and empathetic simulation game where the player takes on the role of a special ed kindergarten teacher. The goal of the game is to manage the daily kindergarten routine, guide the children through learning and play stations, and provide precise responses to each child's emotional needs. The game challenges players with real time planning, prioritization and emotional regulation creating a supportive enviroment.

## Target Platform
PC\Web Browser.



## Formal Elements
1.Players: The target audience are players ages 12 and up, students and professionals in education, therapy as well as fans of cozy and management games.
Number Of players: Single-Player.
Interaction Pattern: Player vs. System.

2.Goals:
Winning Goal-Complete the school week with every child making tangible progress toward their individual developmental milestones while keeping the group's emotional regulation meter high.
Goal Communication- At the start of each day, a visual daily schedule details each child's goals. During gameplay, real-time status meters appear above the children's heads.

3.Procedures:
First 30 Seconds (Hook): The game opens with a warm, colorful morning scene accompanied by soothing background music. The main door opens, and the first child enters. The player receives a simple, highlighted visual prompt to greet the child and guide them to the visual schedule board. This immediate positive feedback creates an instant emotional bond that encourages the player to continue.

Core Loop:
Identify: Monitor children's focus and regulation indicators across the classroom.

Direct: Move the teacher around the classroom and lead children to appropriate stations (crafts area, sensory room, playground).

Execute: Perform short therapeutic/educational mini-games (e.g., shape matching, breathing exercises, fine motor skill practice).

Regulate & Support: Recognize early signs of sensory overload and provide appropriate soothing tools.
End Procedure: At the end of each day, the teacher gathers the children for a wrap-up circle, says goodbye, and reviews a "Daily Progress Report" showing developmental achievements and empathy scores earned.

Controls & Interaction:
Keyboard (WASD / Arrow Keys): Teacher movement around the room.

Mouse (Click & Drag): Selecting objects, handing out regulation tools, and completing mini-games.

Learning the Procedures: Day 1 functions as an interactive tutorial featuring highlighted visual cues and brief animated demonstrations.

4.Rules
Restrictive Rules:
The teacher can only assist one child at a time.
The calming/sensory room capacity is limited to two children simultaneously.
Leaving a child in sensory overload without support past a time limit results in class-wide disruption.

Outcome Rules:
Prompt and accurate care increases the child's regulation meter and awards development points.
Ignoring signs of distress causes sensory overload to spread to nearby children and drains the teacher's patience meter.
Learning the Rules: Contextual tooltips, immediate visual/audio feedback for every action, and an accessible in-game digital guide in the menu.

5.Resources
Resource Types:
Time: Defines the active window for completing tasks.

Regulation Tools (Noise-canceling headphones, weighted lap pads, visual communication cards): Items that help soothe children quickly.

Teacher Energy / Patience Meter: A resource that allows the teacher to move quickly and handle high-stress situations efficiently.

Resource Utility: Soothing tools prevent meltdowns, while high teacher energy ensures quick reaction times.

Resource Acquisition: Organizing and tidying the classroom at the end of the day grants a budget to unlock new tools; short breaks in the staff lounge replenish teacher energy.

Resource Scarcity: Soothing tools have cooldown periods or sanitization times between uses; teacher energy drains faster when managing multiple simultaneous sensory events.

UI Display: A fixed top bar displays available tools, remaining teacher energy, and an analog classroom clock.

6.Conflicts
Player vs. System (Obstacles): Random environmental disruptions, such as loud construction noise from outside, sudden weather changes canceling outdoor play, or unexpected overlaps in children's needs.
Player vs. Self (Dilemmas): Time vs. Empathy prioritization — Should you pause a structured group activity to provide extended 1-on-1 support to a single frustrated child, or stick to the schedule so the rest of the class isn't delayed?

7.Boundaries
World Structure: Closed, flat 2D environment (Top-down / Isometric view).

Visualizing Boundaries: Classroom walls, playground fencing, and the main entrance door clearly mark the playable boundaries.

Map Design Principles:

Meaning: Every area serves a distinct therapeutic purpose (Playground for motor skills, Sensory room for calming, Crafts corner for cognitive focus).

Mobility: Wide, obstacle-free pathways ensuring smooth navigation for both teacher and children.

Orientation: Distinct color coding for each zone alongside clear visual signage.

Interest: Interactive elements packed into every station (ball pit, drawing station, outdoor play gear).

Guidance: Designed floor markings and visual cues guiding children to their next scheduled activity.

8.Outcomes
Possible Outcomes: 3 distinct outcomes:

Full Success: All children reached their daily developmental goals and the classroom atmosphere remained calm and positive.

Partial Success: Most goals were met, but several sensory overloads were resolved late, causing minor schedule delays.

Burnout / Reset: The teacher's patience meter fully depleted due to widespread sensory overload, ending the day early to regroup.

Skill vs. Luck: Approximately 80% skill (planning, time management, fast reaction) and 20% luck (timing of random environmental events).

Game Type: Complex (an ongoing management and nurturing simulation that is non-zero-sum).
